Subject: The payment gateway decision most startups get wrong

Most gateway comparisons rank options by processing fees, then show you a feature table. For a simple e-commerce store, that's fine. For a growth-stage company, it's almost useless.

The real issue is total cost of ownership over two to three years — not the headline rate. A 0.1% fee advantage can evaporate quickly when you're re-tokenizing stored cards, rebuilding webhooks, and absorbing hundreds of thousands in engineering hours during a forced migration.

--- EMAIL 2 (Consideration, send 4 days later) ---
Subject: Why your payment gateway choice gets harder as you scale

There's a pattern we see repeatedly: a startup picks a fully integrated platform because it was fast to ship, then hits a wall at scale — authorization rates nobody benchmarked, 3DS2 flows killing conversion in European markets, manual dispute handling eating engineering time.

The problem usually isn't the original decision. It's that the decision wasn't made with 18-month volume and geography in mind.

At Proficient, when we work through payment infrastructure with growth-stage teams, we start by identifying which category of gateway actually fits the business model — integrated platform, direct acquiring, orchestration, or vertical-specific. That narrows a field of ten options to two or three real candidates.

The category fit question is the one most evaluation processes skip entirely.
