Subject: You're probably overpaying on card processing fees

Every time a customer pays by card, three parties take a cut before the money reaches you: the issuing bank, the card network, and your processor. Most founders see one blended number on their statement and move on.

That blended rate is hiding something. At $500K in annual revenue, the gap between an optimised setup and an unexamined one can be $15,000 a year. At $5M, it's six figures.

The fees aren't all fixed. Some are set by networks, some are negotiable, and all of them respond to decisions you're already making about your tech stack.

--- EMAIL 2 (Consideration, send 4 days later) ---
Subject: The one part of your processing fee you can actually negotiate

Interchange — the largest slice of your processing cost — is set by the card networks. You can't negotiate it directly. But you can influence which rate category your transactions fall into, and that distinction matters more than most founders realise.

Submitting Level 2 or Level 3 transaction data on B2B payments, for example, can reduce interchange by 30–50 basis points. That's often a one-time engineering change with compounding returns every month after.

The processor margin, on the other hand, is fully negotiable — especially above $100K per month in volume. If you're on flat-rate or tiered pricing, you're almost certainly subsidising other merchants.
