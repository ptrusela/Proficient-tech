Most AI automation advice will cost you money before it saves you any.

The gap between vendor promises and production reality is wide. Closing it requires less focus on the models themselves and more on what surrounds them: process selection, evaluation frameworks, observable pipelines, and clear criteria for when to expand autonomy and when to hold back.

The practical starting point is treating AI automation as a spectrum. Rule-based workflows handle the deterministic cases. Model-assisted workflows cut the cognitive load on classification, extraction, and drafting tasks. Autonomous agents tackle multi-step problems — but only once the underlying data and evaluation infrastructure is ready. Conflating these three layers is where most implementations get expensive.

Process selection matters as much as model choice. High volume, well-defined outputs, existing data history, and tolerance for measured error rates are the signals that a workflow is worth engineering. Impressive demos that fail unpredictably in production erode trust faster than any efficiency gain can recover.

For startups handling payments, the stakes are higher still. Financial workflows carry auditability and explainability requirements that need to be designed in from the start, not retrofitted when a compliance question surfaces.
